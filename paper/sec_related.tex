This paper sits at the intersection of three literatures, and the honest statement of
what is new requires being precise about each.

\paragraph{Streaming second-order methods with $O(dN)$ cost.}
Inversion-free stochastic Newton methods maintain a running inverse curvature estimate by
rank-one Sherman--Morrison updates \citep{sherman1950adjustment} and have been developed
for logistic regression \citep{bercu2020efficient}, Gauss--Newton problems
\citep{cenac2025efficient}, the geometric median
\citep{godichonbaggioni2024online}, and general conditioned stochastic gradient descent
\citep{leluc2023asymptotic,boyer2023asymptotic}; \citet{chau2024inversion} survey the
inversion-free idea more broadly, and \citet{byrd2016stochastic,wang2017stochastic,moritz2016linearly}
give the stochastic quasi-Newton and L-BFGS counterparts. Our starting point is
\citet{godichonbaggioni2025adaptive}, who thin the rank-one updates with a Bernoulli
variable and take mini-batches of size $d$ to reach $O(dN)$ total cost with asymptotic
efficiency, and \citet{godichonbaggioni2026complexity}, who reach the same budget by
masking Hessian columns. \textbf{The $O(dN)$ budget and the idea of skipping curvature
updates are theirs, not ours}; \citet{chen2024online} independently thin Hessian
\emph{entries} with probability $O(1/d^2)$ in a zeroth-order method. All of this work assumes independent
observations. Most of it proves central limit theorems and stops there, but not all: for
averaged first-order algorithms under independent data \citet{godichonbaggioni2019online}
gives a recursive online estimator of the asymptotic variance with almost sure and $L^2$
rates, and \citet{chen2020statistical,zhu2023online,godichonbaggioni2024online} supply
plug-in and batch-means constructions. \textbf{Online estimation of the
\emph{instantaneous} sandwich under independent data is therefore not new either}; what is
missing is the long-run object under dependence, and the streaming second-order setting. Our contribution on this axis is
(i) the analysis of the rank-one recursion when the accumulated terms are dependent,
(ii) replacing Bernoulli thinning by a deterministic gap, which is strictly better at
matched cost (\Cref{prop:gap}), and (iii) turning the central limit theorem into usable
intervals.

\paragraph{Online inference for stochastic approximation.}
Under independent sampling this is a mature area.
\citet{chen2020statistical} give the plug-in and increasing-batch-means variance
estimators for averaged stochastic gradient descent; \citet{zhu2023online} make batch
means fully recursive with growing overlapping windows and obtain
$\|\widehat\Sigma-\Sigma\|=O(N^{-1/8})$; \citet{singh2025utility} show equal batch sizes
are preferable and apply the flat-top bias correction; \citet{ni2026online} improve the
batch-means rate to $N^{-1/6}$ and establish the minimax rate
$\Theta(N^{-(1-a)/2})$ for \emph{Hessian-free} covariance estimation from the iterate path;
\citet{wei2026refining} give a fully online de-biased estimator that needs no second-order
information and reaches $N^{(a-1)/2}\sqrt{\log N}$, i.e.\ that minimax rate up to a logarithm,
so the Hessian-free class is close to saturated. \citet{fang2018online,fang2019scalable} bootstrap;
\citet{su2023higrad} split the trajectory; \citet{lee2022fast,li2026stream,chen2024online}
studentise by a random scaling matrix and estimate nothing.
\citet{leung2026principles} and \citet{wu2009recursive} design recursive long-run
variance estimators directly. \textbf{None of these is a claim we duplicate};
what we take from them is the batch-means template and the flat-top correction, and what
we add is the block-\emph{gradient} construction and its consequence for the rate
(\Cref{rem:whyfast}).

Two recent papers give online covariance estimation for streaming \emph{second-order}
methods: \citet{kuang2026online} for a sketched Newton method and
\citet{wang2026inference} for Nesterov-accelerated sketching. Both are under independent
data, both target the instantaneous sandwich, and both require the gradient noise to be a
martingale difference with respect to the algorithm's filtration
(\citealp[Assumption 3.2]{kuang2026online}) --- exactly the condition that temporal
dependence destroys. \citet{kuang2026online} remark that their analysis extends to
conditioned stochastic gradient methods generally, which would include an
independent-data version of our algorithm; the martingale assumption is what keeps that
remark from covering the present setting.

\paragraph{Stochastic approximation on dependent data.}
Convergence of stochastic approximation under Markovian or mixing noise is classical
\citep{benveniste1990adaptive,kushner2003stochastic,borkar2008stochastic,andrieu2005stability,fort2015central,karimi2019nonasymptotic},
and modern rates are available for Markov-chain gradient descent
\citep{sun2018markov,doan2020convergence,even2023stochastic,duchi2012ergodic,nagaraj2020least,kowshik2021streaming,agarwal2013generalization}.
\citet{godichonbaggioni2023learning} analyse streaming stochastic gradient methods with
time-dependent and biased gradients, and identify blocked or growing mini-batches as the
device that breaks dependence; they give $L^2$ rates but no central limit theorem and no
inference. \textbf{Blocked gradients as a dependence-breaking device are theirs.}

On the inference side, \citet{liu2023mixing} give a multiplier bootstrap for
$\phi$-mixing streams using alternating growing blocks, and establish the long-run
sandwich limit and, qualitatively, the failure of dependence-blind intervals;
\citet{li2023markovian} give a functional central limit theorem and the semiparametric
efficiency bound $H^{-1}SH^{-1}$ under Markovian sampling, with random scaling for
inference; \citet{samsonov2025statistical} give Berry--Esseen bounds and an
overlapping-batch-means variance for \emph{linear} stochastic approximation with Markovian
noise; \citet{roy2023online} give an online batch-means estimator of the Markovian
long-run sandwich; \citet{li2026stream} treat controlled Markov chains with random scaling.
\textbf{The long-run sandwich limit, the efficiency bound, and the existence of online
long-run covariance estimators under dependence are all prior art} --- for first-order
methods. Every one of these works is first-order; none maintains a curvature inverse, and
the two that estimate a long-run covariance do so from the iterate path, at
$O(N^{-1/8})$, with block lengths that must grow like $N^{3/4}$
(\citealp[Prop.~2, Cor.~2]{samsonov2025statistical}). \Cref{rem:whyfast} explains why
block gradients escape that constraint, and \Cref{thm:lrv} quantifies the gain.

\paragraph{Long-run variance estimation.}
The statistical device is heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent covariance
estimation \citep{newey1987simple,andrews1991heteroskedasticity,andrews1992improved,newey1994automatic,white1980hetero,white1984asymptotic},
its fixed-$b$ alternative \citep{kiefer2002heteroskedasticity,kiefer2005new,sun2008optimal},
and the batch-means literature from simulation output analysis and Markov chain Monte
Carlo \citep{glynn1991estimating,glynn1992asymptotic,damerdji1994strong,flegal2010batch,jones2006fixed,vats2019multivariate}.
The two-scale correction we use is the flat-top lag window of
\citet{politis1995bias}, refined by \citet{politis2011higher} and recast as ``lugsail''
lag windows by \citet{vats2022lugsail}; \citet{singh2025utility} bring it to stochastic
gradient descent under independent data, where their Eqs.~(15) and (17) coincide with our
\eqref{eq:Sft} and their Corollary~1 shows the convergence \emph{rate} is unchanged.
\textbf{We do not claim the correction.} We claim the bias order it attains at
block-gradient resolution under geometric mixing, which is the property that makes
$b\asymp\log N$ admissible, and the streaming implementation.
Block bootstrap and subsampling \citep{kunsch1989jackknife,politis1999subsampling,lahiri2003resampling}
are the resampling counterparts.

\paragraph{Asymptotics for dependent $M$-estimation.}
The population statements we use are standard: sandwich asymptotics for $M$-estimators and
generalised method of moments \citep{huber1967behavior,hansen1982gmm,white1982mle,vandervaart1998asymptotic},
misspecified models with dependent observations \citep{domowitz1982misspecified}, marginal
models for correlated responses \citep{liang1986longitudinal,zeger1986longitudinal} --- the
setting our logistic design imitates --- and central limit theorems for mixing sequences
\citep{rosenblatt1956central,ibragimov1962some,herrndorf1984functional,wooldridge1988invariance,doukhan1994mixing,bradley2005basic,merlevede2006recent,rio2017asymptotic,dedecker2007weak}.
The technical inputs to our proofs are Davydov's covariance inequality
\citep{davydov1968convergence,rio1993covariance}, the Bernstein-type inequality for weakly
dependent sequences of \citet{merlevede2011bernstein}, the almost sure behaviour of
stochastic algorithms \citep{pelletier1998almost,pelletier2000asymptotic,fabian1968asymptotic,mokkadem2011generalization,robbins1971convergence},
and the functional central limit theorem of \citet{herrndorf1984functional}.
