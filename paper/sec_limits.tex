%% main text can stay short without dropping anything a reviewer would want to see.
%% Shared by ../paper/main.tex and ../submission/body.tex.
We list the boundaries of the result plainly, because several of them are load-bearing.
\Cref{app:limits} discusses each at length.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(L\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Memory is $O(d^2)$, not $O(d)$.} The $O(dN)$ claim is about \emph{time}; for $d$ in the
thousands memory is the binding constraint, and whether a block-gradient long-run covariance survives a
sketched curvature representation is open.
\item \textbf{Geometric mixing is a real restriction.} It is what buys $b\asymp\log N$; under algebraic
mixing the admissible $b$ grows, and long-memory streams are outside the theory. The diagnostic $r_j$
detects an inadequate $b$ but does not remove it, and we claim no automatic rule for choosing $b$.
\item \textbf{$\Sft$ need not be positive semidefinite}, as for all flat-top and lugsail estimators
\citep[Remark~5]{singh2025utility}; marginal intervals need only the scalars $u_j^{\!\top}\Sft u_j$, the
documented fallback triggers on $\RealPsdFallback$ of coordinate-replicate pairs on the real streams and
never in simulation, and a user who needs the full matrix must project it.
\item \textbf{The curvature form is required.} It holds for generalised linear models, ridge and softmax
regression, the geometric median and Gauss--Newton problems, but not for a general objective; without it
there is no rank-one recursion.
\item \textbf{The warm-up is a requirement, not an implementation detail}, and $c_{\mathrm w}$ is a
tuning constant the asymptotic theory is silent about; what sets its size is the number of effectively
distinct design points it spans, and it is why the honest time bound is $O(dN+c_{\mathrm w}d^3)$.
\item \textbf{Blocking creates a stability condition, and our fix is not fully proved.}
\Cref{prop:shift} holds on the event that the safeguard is eventually inactive, and we do not prove that
event has probability one, because the safeguard is not predictable; we report activation counts instead
($2$ to $7$ per run, flat in $N$). The condition itself comes from a linearisation, so it is exact only
for linear models, and $c_D$ has a genuine failure mode on the one design that needs it.
\item \textbf{The central limit theorem is proved for fixed $b$, and we recommend growing $b$.} At
$b\asymp\log N$ the proof needs the constants of \Cref{lem:series} to be bounded in probability uniformly
in $N$; we state that hypothesis in \Cref{thm:clt} rather than hide it, and we do not verify it.
\item \textbf{Rates we do not prove.} We give an almost sure rate and a central limit theorem, not a
Berry--Esseen bound \citep{samsonov2025statistical}; we attain the efficiency bound of
\citet{li2023markovian} rather than establishing it; our rate is not comparable to the minimax rate for
\emph{Hessian-free} covariance estimation from an iterate path \citep{ni2026online} because we use more
information; and we claim no optimality within our own information set.
\item \textbf{Scope of the evidence.} All designs are convex $M$-estimation with $d\le160$ and
$N\le4\times10^5$; there is no deep-learning experiment and the theory would not support one. On the real
streams we do not know the truth, which is why we run two protocols with complementary weaknesses.
\item \textbf{One thing we deliberately do not claim.} Gapping improves the curvature estimate at matched
cost, but curvature enters the limit law only through a rate, so the end-to-end effect on coverage is
second order and we could not measure it reliably.
\end{enumerate}
